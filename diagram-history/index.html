Boxes, Lines and Arrows

A short history of diagrams, from clay tablets to Mermaid and Pele, and the stories behind their names

The world is complex, dynamic, multidimensional; the paper is static, flat. How are we to represent the rich visual world of experience and measurement on mere flatland?
Edward Tufte, Envisioning Information, 1990

A diagram is a picture of something you cannot see: a relation, a process, an argument, a structure. A painting of Königsberg shows bridges; Euler’s diagram of Königsberg shows only which land touches which, and that was enough to found a branch of mathematics. This page follows that idea across 2,600 years, in four eras, ending with the newest turn: diagrams written as plain text and drawn by a machine, which is where Mermaid, Pele and a Nim library to come all live.

I. Pictures of thought before 1700

  • 600 BCE The Babylonian Map of the World puts Babylon inside a ring of ocean, with triangular regions beyond. It is a diagram of a worldview rather than a survey.British Museum, object BM 92687. Clay tablet from Sippar.
  • 300 BCE Euclid’s Elements. The lettered figure is part of the proof: the text says “let AB be…” and the reader finds A and B on the drawing. Those labels are the ancestors of the node ids in every diagram language.Netz, The Shaping of Deduction in Greek Mathematics (1999), argues the lettered diagram is what made Greek deduction work.
  • c. 270 Porphyry’s Isagoge splits substance into body and spirit, body into living and not, down to the individual. Medieval copyists drew it as a branching figure, the arbor porphyriana, the first famous tree diagram.Lima, The Book of Trees (2014), traces the tree form from Porphyry to phylogenies.
  • c. 1000 An anonymous scribe plots the paths of the planets against time on a grid, in a copy of Macrobius. It is the oldest known time-series chart, and Tufte reproduces it at the start of his first book.Funkhouser, “A note on a tenth century graph”, Osiris 1 (1936). Tufte, Visual Display, p. 28.
  • c. 1305 Ramon Llull’s Ars uses concentric paper wheels, each marked with concepts, turned to generate every combination. The diagram becomes a machine for thinking.Gardner, Logic Machines and Diagrams (1958), ch. 1.
  • c. 1350 Nicole Oresme draws the intensity of a quality (heat, speed) as vertical lines over a base line, and sees that uniformly increasing speed makes a triangle. It is a graph three centuries before Descartes.
  • 1626 Christoph Scheiner’s Rosa Ursina shows the sun’s spots day after day as a row of identical discs: small multiples, the form Tufte later praised by name.
  • 1637 Descartes’ La Géométrie joins algebra and geometry with coordinates. 1686: Halley maps the trade winds with strokes on a world map.

II. Statistics and logic find their forms 1700–1900

  • 1736 Euler shows the seven bridges of Königsberg cannot be crossed once each. He throws away the map and keeps only land masses and connections: the first graph.Euler, “Solutio problematis ad geometriam situs pertinentis” (written 1736, published 1741).
  • 1765 Joseph Priestley’s Chart of Biography draws 2,000 lives as horizontal bars on a time axis, the ancestor of the timeline and of the Gantt chart.
  • 1768 In letters to a German princess, Euler explains syllogisms with nested and overlapping circles.
  • 1786 William Playfair’s Commercial and Political Atlas introduces the line chart and the bar chart; his Statistical Breviary (1801) adds the pie chart. Nearly all of business graphics comes from one Scottish engineer.Playfair, The Commercial and Political Atlas (1786). Reprint ed. Wainer & Spence (2005).
  • 1854 John Snow marks cholera deaths around the Broad Street pump in Soho. 1858: Florence Nightingale’s polar-area “rose” shows that more soldiers died of disease than of wounds.
  • 1859 The only figure in Darwin’s Origin of Species is a tree diagram of branching descent.
  • 1869 Charles Joseph Minard’s map of Napoleon’s Russian campaign. A band whose width is the size of the army thins from 422,000 to 10,000, with the temperature of the retreat plotted below it. Tufte called it perhaps the best statistical graphic ever drawn.Tufte, Visual Display (1983), pp. 40–41.
  • 1879 Frege’s Begriffsschrift writes logic as two-dimensional branching strokes. 1880: John Venn’s overlapping circles show every possible combination of classes. c. 1896: Peirce’s existential graphs do logic by drawing.Venn, “On the diagrammatic and mechanical representation of propositions and reasonings”, Philosophical Magazine (1880).

III. Diagrams of process and systems 1900–1980

  • 1896 Karol Adamiecki’s harmonogram schedules steel works; c. 1912 Henry Gantt popularises the bar schedule that still bears his name.
  • 1921 Frank and Lillian Gilbreth present “Process Charts” to the American Society of Mechanical Engineers: standard symbols for each step of work, the first flowcharts.Gilbreth & Gilbreth, “Process Charts: First Steps in Finding the One Best Way to Do Work”, ASME (1921).
  • 1925 Otto Neurath and Gerd Arntz in Vienna begin Isotype: counts drawn as rows of identical pictograms.
  • 1933 Harry Beck’s London Underground diagram ignores geography. Lines run only horizontal, vertical or at 45°, and stations are spaced evenly. Riders loved it, and every transit map since has copied it.Garland, Mr Beck’s Underground Map (1994).
  • 1947 Goldstine and von Neumann draw “flow diagrams” to plan programs for the first stored-program computers. The flowchart moves from factories to software.Goldstine & von Neumann, Planning and Coding of Problems for an Electronic Computing Instrument (1947).
  • 1949 Richard Feynman’s diagrams turn integrals in quantum field theory into lines and squiggles that physicists can sketch and compute from.Kaiser, Drawing Theories Apart (2005).
  • 1962 Carl Adam Petri’s nets model concurrency. 1967: Jacques Bertin’s Sémiologie graphique gives the first grammar of visual variables: position, size, value, texture, colour, orientation, shape.
  • 1976 Peter Chen’s entity-relationship model: boxes, diamonds and crow’s feet for databases.Chen, “The Entity-Relationship Model: Toward a Unified View of Data”, ACM TODS 1(1) (1976).

IV. Diagrams written as text 1980–today

The newest change is not a new shape but a new way to make shapes. You describe the structure in words and an algorithm decides where everything goes. Two ideas made that possible: a layout algorithm good enough to trust, and a syntax small enough to type in a chat message or a README.

  • 1981 Sugiyama, Tagawa and Toda publish the layered method for drawing hierarchies: assign layers, reduce crossings, place nodes. Nearly every flowchart renderer since, Mermaid’s and Pele’s included, descends from it.Sugiyama, Tagawa & Toda, “Methods for Visual Understanding of Hierarchical System Structures”, IEEE Trans. SMC 11(2) (1981).
  • 1982 Brian Kernighan’s PIC, a troff preprocessor: box; arrow; box becomes a picture in a typeset Unix manual.Kernighan, “PIC — A Language for Typesetting Graphics”, Software: Practice and Experience 12 (1982).
  • 1983 Tufte self-publishes The Visual Display of Quantitative Information: data-ink, chartjunk, small multiples. 1987: David Harel’s statecharts.
  • c. 1990 dot and Graphviz at AT&T Bell Labs. digraph { a -> b } becomes the lingua franca of machine-drawn graphs.Gansner, Koutsofios, North & Vo, “A Technique for Drawing Directed Graphs”, IEEE TSE 19(3) (1993).
  • 1997 UML is standardised by the OMG, merging the notations of Booch, Rumbaugh and Jacobson. c. 2005: Till Tantau’s TikZ for LaTeX.
  • 2009 PlantUML draws UML from plain text, using Graphviz for layout.
  • 2014 Knut Sveidqvist starts Mermaid after losing a Visio file he needed to update. It is JavaScript, so it renders in the browser, and its syntax looks like Markdown.Mermaid (software), Wikipedia; Pulse 2.0 interview with Sveidqvist (2024).
  • 2020 D. Richard Hipp revives PIC as Pikchr, for the Fossil and SQLite docs.
  • 2022 GitHub renders Mermaid blocks in Markdown, and Mermaid becomes the default way to put a diagram in a README. The same year Terrastruct open-sources D2.GitHub blog, “Include diagrams in your Markdown files with Mermaid”, 14 Feb 2022.
  • 2026 Obsidian releases Pele, which renders Mermaid syntax to SVG with no runtime dependencies, synchronously, with colours and fonts as CSS variables. Mermaid stays the language; Pele is a second, faster renderer for it.github.com/obsidianmd/pele, MIT licence. Supports every diagram type in Mermaid 12.1.0.

The same two-box graph, in the languages of this lineage, shows how much the syntax has shrunk:

PIC 1982

box "A"; arrow; box "B"

dot c. 1990

digraph { A -> B }

TikZ c. 2005

\node (a) {A};
\node[right=of a] (b) {B};
\draw[->] (a) -- (b);

PlantUML 2009

@startuml
A -> B
@enduml

Mermaid 2014

flowchart LR
  A --> B

Pikchr 2020

box "A"; arrow; box "B"

D2 2022

A -> B

Pele 2026

flowchart LR
  A --> B

Pele reads the Mermaid language unchanged; the new part is the renderer.

What’s in a name

Diagram tools get named in three ways. Some say what they do (dot, PIC, D2). Some make a joke (TikZ, Pikchr). And some are named for a story, which is where both Mermaid and Pele belong.

NameWhere it comes from
MermaidWhen Knut Sveidqvist got home with the idea, his children were watching The Little Mermaid on TV. “The name just stuck in my head.”
PelePele, the Hawaiian goddess of volcanoes and fire, according to the project site as reported by third-party write-ups. The repository’s tagline keeps the joke: “Renders Mermaid diagrams faster than you’d run from a volcano! 🌋” No source mentions Pelé the footballer, whose name has an accent.
dotThe language of Graphviz (graph visualization); a graph is dots joined by lines.
PICShort for picture, next to its troff siblings eqn and tbl.
TikZA recursive German acronym: TikZ ist kein Zeichenprogramm, “TikZ is not a drawing program”.
PikchrPronounced “picture”: PIC’s heir, spelled to be searchable.
D2Short for Declarative Diagramming.

So the chain behind a Nim wrapper is a fairy tale (a mermaid) handed to a volcano goddess (Pele). Whatever comes next can pick up either thread, or start its own.
